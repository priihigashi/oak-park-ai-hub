## RECOMMENDATION: Split into 2 parts (7 + 13 slides)

Go with the split the production notes propose. Here's why:

The 7/13 break hits a natural story cliff. Part 1 (slides 1–7) ends exactly where the historical story becomes personal and present: "$124 trillion is changing hands right now — mostly to people already rich." That's the cliffhanger that makes someone follow for Part 2. If you run all 20 slides in one carousel, most viewers drop off before they reach the contractor example (slide 11) and the AI section (slide 17), which are two of the strongest hooks. Splitting lets each part carry its own compelling arc.

Part 1 arc: "Here's where the money came from — and why none of it was a fair race." (History → slavery as capital → inheritance → the wealth transfer happening now.)

Part 2 arc: "Here's how it works TODAY — and why this affects your paycheck, your rent, and your business." (Policy capture → tax tricks → the contractor example → monopoly → AI → what to do about it.)

The 13-slide Part 2 is on the longer side, but FORMAT-021 (educational explainer) audiences tolerate depth when the hook is specific. Slide 11 (the contractor example — "Imagine one company that owns the lumber yard and the construction firm") is a strong Part 2 opener because it's hyper-specific and unexpected in a macro-economics carousel.
