# Detailing

## The four control layers
Every envelope junction keeps four layers continuous:

| Layer | Job |
|---|---|
| Water | Rain and the drainage path |
| Air | The airtight line |
| Vapour | Control on the warm side, as the climate requires |
| Thermal | The insulation line |

A detail succeeds when each layer can be traced around the junction without lifting the pen. This is the red-pen test: the junior does it by hand on every detail before the agent drafts it.

## Junctions that fail most often
- Window head, sill and jamb.
- Wall to roof and wall to floor.
- Balcony and canopy penetrations (thermal bridges).
- The base of the wall at ground level.
- Movement joints.

## Rules
- **Real build-ups.** The detail's build-up matches the model's wall, floor or roof type: the same layers, thicknesses and materials. A detail that disagrees with its type is a coordination error.
- **Condensation.** Check interstitial condensation for new build-ups, using the Glaser method or a dynamic hygrothermal simulation where the build-up is unusual. Name the method and the climate data.
- **Tolerances.** Draw the gap, the sealant joint and the fixing, not ideal lines.
- **References.** Every detail is referenced from a plan or section, by callout or keynote.
- **Escalation.** Details involving structure, fire-stopping or waterproofing warranties go to the senior.
