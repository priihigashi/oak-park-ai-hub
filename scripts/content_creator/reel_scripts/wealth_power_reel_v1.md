Go back far enough and the richest people almost never got there from one good job. They owned the thing everyone else needed — the land, the factories, the mines — and money flowed to them while other people did the work.

By 1860, the four million enslaved people in the US were the single largest financial asset in the entire American economy. More than all the factories and railroads combined. That money didn't vanish after the Civil War. It got banked, invested, and passed down.

Nobody starts the race at zero. One kid is born with good schools, no debt, and help buying a house. Another works just as hard — from a mile back.

Right now, $124 trillion is about to transfer between generations. The wealthiest two percent will capture more than half of it.

And that money doesn't just buy yachts. It buys lobbyists, lawmakers, and the rules themselves. Money creates power. Power protects the money. Protected money creates even more power.

A teacher pays tax every single paycheck. The 25 richest Americans paid a true tax rate of 3.4%.

Now ask the same question about AI. Three companies already control 63% of the world's cloud. The same pattern — same game, same players.

This was never about envy. The question is whether the game is fair.
