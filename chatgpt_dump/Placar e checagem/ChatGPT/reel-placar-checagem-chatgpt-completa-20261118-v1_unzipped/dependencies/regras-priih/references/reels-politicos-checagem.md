# Political / fact-check reels and posts (her rules, verbatim from memory)

- ChatGPT fact-check prompts: only check facts, don't hunt for arguments defending any side; report ONLY what is wrong. For content about attacks on women, don't defend sexist speech or add mitigating context. Review its audits critically: it sometimes defends politicians without full info, trusts false/incomplete/repeated articles, adds unnecessary caveats.
- Facts harmful to women: never defend or minimize; ask how bad it would be for women if minimized; choose between stating it, a truly needed caveat, or explaining how problematic it is.
- Political charts: right-wing government periods get dashed lines at start/end plus a very light blue background; extend the who-governed timeline back to earlier decades when the other side omits that the right controlled the state.
- Don't just report a vote: investigate context (present? what he said before, freely or under pressure, his plan, proposals signed, likely allies).
- Current president full drawn body = navy suit with trousers, head on collar (not a long black coat). Use the head cutout from the "Vai virar Venezuela?" reel, never the one from "Saidinha".
- Other-country comparisons: give that country's actual figure with source.
- Quote clips: cut to the key line but always show full context underneath (who asked, when, what was said).
- Verdicts judge what the claim makes the audience conclude. Blaming the successor for something that started before = "Falso". Core is a lie = "Falso" even if context also missing; "Falta contexto" only for true-but-misleading. Imprecision against the speaker's own point isn't "Falso"; one that inflates in their favor leans "Falso". Same rule for both sides. Only flag a fact-checker's problem if verified; no artificial balancing. Be flexible with close numbers (130 vs 134 = true). Separate labels for partly true / fact + opinion.
- Huge money figures: chart for scale + relatable reference (how many houses it buys).
- A politician's excuse: say whether proven; if not, "não há prova, só a palavra dele". Avoid "would you accept this from the other side?" closers.
- When a politician dodges: show him talking (fast-forwarded, last line at normal speed) before saying the question went unanswered.
- Reels built on a speaker she backs: data is support only; true points stamped "Verdade"; no opinion/joke labels that pull off the point.
- Placar reels: "Provavelmente" only on screen; narration says just "Fake." / "Meio termo." / "Verdade." One scoreboard per person.
- Debunking a politician's posts: check whether he/his side voted for or against the very thing (nominal votes); no nominal vote = don't claim a position. Short "alfinetada" on his posture.
- Research tips (e.g. AI prompt) go at the end, not the opening.
- Debunk of a creator she disagrees with: don't name or credit him, no @, no collab, show him few times; neutral chip "Vídeo da direita". Never share the @ of anyone spreading disinformation anywhere.
- First comments: few strong sources only (long lists make Meta cut reach). Direct official links, never bit.ly.
- Appointed officials (STF etc.): show who appointed them or that they were elected; remind that appointment is a Senate vote; take the right's view of the group seriously.
- Trial reels of an already-posted video need a changed caption. Trial reels get no collabs.
- Vet regular people before featuring them (is the right trying to discredit them? anything wrong?).
- Pros/cons explainers: every point gets an extra sentence on why it's good or bad.
- Reference videos: go through every one and pull all critique points; they're for ideas/facts only, don't feature the people in them and never copy their example, analogy or opening line.
- ChatGPT verification prompts: don't give our conclusion; include sources found (described by data), tell it to research on its own first, then check ours without limiting itself; don't refuse for lack of exact numbers, give best estimate. Cover story AND visuals in one shot (per scene "corrigir isso / colocar isso"), don't change the story much. Never rewrite our ROTEIRO: its own version goes appended at the end under "VERSÃO CHATGPT". Always ask: did we leave out any relevant point? easy wording? any line that invites "você não sabe como isso funciona" comments (misused terms, steps out of order, oversimplification)?
- Whenever relevant (cesta básica etc.), say most of the PL voted against: ~3 main PL faces with a registered vote (prefer popular ones like Nikolas Ferreira); no registered vote = just "PL".
- Estadão page: right-leaning, omits benefits, unbalanced blame; look for what it leaves out.
- Repeat the exaggerations people hear in order to correct them, conversationally ("tem gente dizendo que... mas..."), varied phrasing.
- Everyone a piece names gets a background check (does a committee member vote against the cause? suggest a third person if the one in charge doesn't fit). Unsure name = keep with caveat ("não temos certeza"), don't remove. Being Bolsonarista is an extra flag that shapes analysis.
- Investigating who's behind a campaign: "works with both sides"/"no public money" proves nothing; dig into companies/business people; a piece that omits what the current government did can be an implicit attack. Each person found gets a card ("caguetar"), confirmed facts only, caveat tone.
- Citizen letters: address who's responsible, CC a trusted person who cares (prefer left or non-Bolsonarista right); check Placar do Congresso for top parliamentarians.
- Israel/Palestine: ChatGPT tends to side with Zionism, extra care. Alternatives: look first at left proposals; right-wing ones only if really good; wary of strongly Bolsonarista authors (e.g. Damares), can be shown as negative "vote NÃO".
- Placar/fact-check method: define the accusation in the opening; find the person's real goal/position; check whether all elements together prove it (not just each true alone); hunt for omitted data; apply cognitive dissonance (how the opposing side would check it, what new arguments); then act as judge weighing support, data, studies, results. When numbers are true but conclusion isn't: "tem 3 verdades, mas a conclusão tá errada".
- Proposals/systems (e.g. electoral reform): check whether other countries tried it and what happened.
- Core mission: the press covers decisions one by one; "o nosso trabalho é pegar, achar e colocar junto essas informações" (connect separate decisions, licitações, bills).
- Experts/commentators/outlets: investigate bias and interests ("um sincerão"); profile card on who they are (start or end), then callback ("lembra que eu falei que ele é isso?"); TV mostly invites market economists.
- Critiquing left voices her audience admires (Jones Manoel, Glauber Braga): very respectful, acknowledge what they get right, critique level and proportion. Don't create a video just because; if weak, say so.
- Technically true but irrelevant lines from the other side ("eu era o juiz do caso") don't earn "Verdade" or video time; weightier context first.
- Fiscal/budget comparisons: like with like (same program, amount actually paid, both governments); check government sites before calling a cut negative; explain budget voted as a package when Congress cut it.
- Never soften politicians or "balance" a negative fact with weak info; judge from the full record. Never say an institution "engavetou" without naming who (relator, committee, house president).
- Party proposals: read the actual document (plano de governo on TSE/site).
- Fact-checks of Flávio/the right: explain what it means for the viewer and briefly compare with Bolsonaro's record and the current government's on that topic.
- Unprovable accusations: show who accused, when (e.g. months before an election), who used it politically.
- Before classifying a claim as unconfirmed/false, check everywhere it could appear (incl. Instagram videos) and what the opposing side says; classify each point as fact, connection, or inference.
- Scandal reels: name main people; say whether the current president is involved, low-key when he isn't.
- US politicians to promote: only those who don't take AIPAC money; never Hakeem Jeffries.
- "Rule of three": back a point with three examples.
